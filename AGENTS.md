# Project Assistant


## What This Project Is

Portfolio risk monitoring and decision-support system. Measures risk, attributes it, detects drift, and produces rebalance recommendations. Whilst insights are important they exist to drive business valuable decisions.

The project is not the software. The project is the business workflow. The software is simply the implementation of part of that workflow.

The goal here is to demonstrate workflow thinking not cosplay an investment mangaement firm.

## Vertical Slices

A thin thread through one specific investment management business workflow, starting from a real business decision and working down the implementation chain. Identify the decision, then we build what is needed to support it.

## Connecting to Career Narative

- Demonstrates I understand risk monitoring for multi-asset portfolios within systematic investment management.
- Show that I can connect risk metrics to specific portfolio decisions and understand the point of risk management, not just the mechanics.
- Show that I think like a PM, not just a developer.
- Show that I understand the limits of quantitative methods
- I'm not trying to show I can build production software, that I have already demonstrated. The focus is using software to support a key decision workflow and domain modelling to show an understanidng of the area.

## Who we are building for

- The framing isn't "I'm a PM." It's: "I built a portfolio risk monitoring system. To validate it, I operated it through a historical market cycle — producing the artifacts, making the decisions a PM would make, and tracking the outcomes. Here's what I learned about what the system needs to actually support investment decisions."
- I'm the quant research engineer who built the risk system, and I validate it by operating as the user.
- This is what good software engineers do — they use their own tools. They eat their own dogfood. They discover what's missing by experiencing the workflow from the other side.
- The PM stance isn't a separate project. It's the test harness for the system I'm building. Every time I run the system through a historical period and make decisions, I'm both: Producing compelling artifacts (the decisions, the outcomes, the lessons). Improving the system (O discover what information I actually needed vs. what the system gave me)
