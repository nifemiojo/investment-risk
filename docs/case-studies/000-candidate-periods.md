For this case study, choose a period where the system output creates a **genuine PM question**, not just an obvious trade.

The best periods are those where:

- risk contributions drift materially;
- the candidate trade is directionally understandable;
- the receiver is not obviously a guaranteed hedge;
- the market regime creates a trade-off or ambiguity;
- the PM interpretation requires more than reading the table.

## Strong candidate periods

### 1. 2022 inflation and rising-rate shock — my recommendation

**Decision date:** Around early or mid-2022  
**Study period:** For example, January–June 2022 or the full 2022 calendar year

This is probably the richest first case study for your portfolio:

```text
SPY / EFA equities
IEF bonds
GLD gold
```

The interesting feature is that the traditional equity–bond diversification relationship became less reliable. Equities and bonds were both pressured by inflation and rising interest rates.

The system might produce a direction such as:

```text
reduce an asset with excess contribution
add IEF because it has a contribution shortfall
```

That creates a good PM question:

> Is IEF genuinely the right receiver simply because its current risk contribution is below target?

The analysis could explore:

- whether the candidate moved the modelled contribution profile closer to target;
- whether adding IEF reduced or increased overall portfolio risk;
- whether IEF actually provided protection during the subsequent period;
- whether the covariance estimate was slow to recognise the changing regime;
- whether restoring the risk budget was desirable when the underlying diversification relationship had changed.

This period is valuable because the system may produce a **reasonable risk-alignment candidate whose investment case is not straightforward**.

Possible case-study thesis:

> The system correctly identified risk-contribution drift, but the obvious receiver was not automatically a reliable diversifier in an inflationary regime. Risk-budget alignment and investment attractiveness were related but distinct questions.

That is a strong PM interpretation.

### 2. COVID crash, February–March 2020 — the clearest first case

**Decision date:** Late January or early February 2020  
**Study period:** February–April 2020

This is the cleanest period for explaining the mechanics.

The portfolio experiences:

- rapid equity losses;
- a sharp change in volatility;
- a flight to quality;
- potentially strong diversification from bonds;
- gold behaving differently from both equities and bonds.

The system output would likely produce a visibly meaningful drift. The case study could ask:

> When risk contribution changes sharply during a fast sell-off, should the PM rebalance immediately, wait for the risk estimate to stabilise, or treat the drift as a consequence of the shock rather than a structural problem?

Interesting PM questions:

- Was the drift caused by weights, volatility, covariance, or all three?
- Did the candidate reduce risk concentration?
- Did the candidate arrive too late because the rolling covariance estimate was backward-looking?
- Would rebalancing into the falling asset have been psychologically and institutionally difficult?
- Did the apparently defensive receiver remain defensive during the relevant window?

Possible thesis:

> A simple risk-drift signal can identify a meaningful concentration change during a crisis, but the signal does not determine whether the appropriate response is to rebalance, tolerate the breach, or reassess the investment regime.

This is easier to communicate than 2022, but perhaps less subtle.

### 3. Q4 2018 equity sell-off

**Decision date:** Around October or November 2018  
**Study period:** October 2018–January 2019

This is a useful shorter episode because:

- equities sold off sharply;
- bonds generally provided diversification;
- gold offered a different defensive exposure;
- the episode was severe but not as historically exceptional as COVID.

The PM question could be:

> Does the candidate trade improve risk alignment while preserving the portfolio’s defensive characteristics?

This period may produce a more straightforward result than 2022. That is useful if you want the first case study to be easy to follow, but it may provide less tension.

Possible thesis:

> The candidate’s immediate impact was directionally consistent with the portfolio’s defensive objective, but the result depended on whether the covariance estimate captured the changing relationship between equities and bonds quickly enough.

---

### 4. 2013 taper tantrum

**Decision date:** Around May or June 2013  
**Study period:** May–August 2013

This is interesting because:

- bonds sold off as rates rose;
- equities did not experience the same type of crisis as 2020;
- gold was weak;
- the conventional assumption that bonds always diversify equity risk was challenged.

The PM question:

> If both equities and bonds are contributing risk during a rates shock, does a risk-contribution rebalance actually improve the portfolio, or merely move weight between two exposed assets?

This is a useful precursor to the 2022 case, although the episode may be less intuitive for a general audience.

---

### 5. 2015–2016 global growth and China concerns

**Decision date:** Around August 2015  
**Study period:** August 2015–February 2016

This period could be interesting for the relationship between:

- SPY and EFA;
- equity risk concentration;
- gold as a potential diversifier;
- bonds as a defensive receiver.

The PM question:

> Does the system identify an opportunity to reduce developed-market equity concentration, or is it reacting to a temporary volatility shock?

This could produce a good analysis of whether the system distinguishes:

```text
persistent risk-budget drift
versus
temporary market stress
```

It may be more complicated to explain than 2020 or 2022.

---

### 6. 2020 recovery and 2021 reopening

**Decision date:** Around late 2020 or early 2021  
**Study period:** Late 2020–mid-2021

This is an interesting contrast to the crash period:

- equities recovered strongly;
- bonds behaved differently as growth and inflation expectations changed;
- gold moved through a different regime;
- risk contributions could shift rapidly without a single obvious crisis.

The PM question:

> Should a PM reduce an asset whose risk contribution has become excessive during a strong rally, even when realised returns and the investment narrative remain favourable?

This would be useful for discussing the difference between:

```text
risk monitoring
versus
return forecasting
```

A high-risk-contribution asset is not necessarily an asset with a poor expected return.

## My recommendation

I would begin with **2022**, unless you want the simplest possible first write-up.

Use a decision date early enough that the system is not merely looking back at the completed crisis. For example:

```text
Decision date: 31 March 2022
Context period: January–March 2022
Review question:
    What does the risk-contribution drift indicate,
    and is the obvious receiver actually attractive
    under the prevailing inflation/rates regime?
```

Then your case study can stay focused on:

### Portfolio context

- What the portfolio is intended to do;
- what the market environment looked like;
- why the risk budget matters.

### System output

- Current contribution;
- target contribution;
- drift;
- tolerance status;
- generated donor and receiver;
- immediate candidate impact.

### PM interpretation

- Whether the candidate addresses the actual portfolio concern;
- whether the receiver is defensible in the prevailing regime;
- what the system does not know;
- whether the evidence supports action, patience, or further investigation.

You do not need to analyse the eventual return outcome in this notebook. That can remain a later historical-operation stage.

## One caution

Avoid selecting a period solely because the eventual market outcome makes the answer obvious.

For example:

```text
IEF rose afterwards, therefore buying IEF was correct.
```

That is hindsight.

The stronger analysis is:

> Given the information visible at the decision date, did the system provide enough evidence to make the candidate worth considering, and what additional PM judgement was still required?

That keeps the case study aligned with your focus:

```text
Portfolio context
    ↓
System output
    ↓
PM interpretation
```
